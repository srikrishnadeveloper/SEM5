# Tips, Common Mistakes and Checklist

## Winner tips for first-timers

### Stage 1 — Internal Hackathon

- Your PPT should be short, clear and structured:
  1. Title and team introduction
  2. Problem statement (read and address every word)
  3. Target users / market
  4. Proposed solution
  5. Technical approach and key features
  6. Demo / prototype (MVP, even if basic)
  7. Existing solutions and why yours is better
  8. Feasibility, impact and future plans
  9. Business model (if applicable)
  10. Challenges and learnings
- A small working prototype adds 10x weight to your idea.
- Do not just submit slides; show something that works.

### Stage 2 — National Portal Submission

- Submit the idea in the official 6-slide PPT template and save as PDF.
- Follow the exact format without changing the slide structure.
- Upload a public GitHub/Bitbucket link if asked.
- Research references and datasets add credibility.
- You may be allowed to apply to multiple PSs; use that strategically.
- Look for PSs with fewer submissions to improve shortlisting odds.

### Stage 3 — Grand Finale

- Build a fully working end-to-end demo. Judges care about seeing it work.
- Listen to mentors carefully and implement their feedback.
- Lead your pitch with the problem, not with features.
- Be honest about what works and what does not. Evaluators can spot exaggeration.
- Keep improving after every review round.
- Practice your final pitch multiple times.

## Common mistakes that lead to rejection

1. **Not addressing the exact PS** — Drifting away from the problem is the fastest way to get rejected.
2. **Overcomplicating the solution** — Simplicity that solves the problem beats complexity that confuses.
3. **PPT overload** — Too much text on slides; use bullets, diagrams and infographics.
4. **No prototype** — A PPT without a demo feels like a dream on paper.
5. **Weak research / no references** — Judges trust evidence, not guesswork.
6. **Cliché ideas** — "AI chatbot," "blockchain for everything," "IoT sensors" without a unique angle are spotted instantly.
7. **Focusing only on tech, not impact** — Explain who benefits and by how much.
8. **Poor role division** — Everyone cannot just code; someone must pitch, design and document.
9. **Ignoring the official template** — Losing points on structure is avoidable.
10. **Over-reliance on AI for content** — Generic AI text is easy to identify; add your own insights.

## Judging / evaluation criteria

Typical parameters used by SIH judges (from official reports and jury guidelines):

1. Novelty of the idea
2. Complexity / technical depth
3. Clarity and details in the prescribed format
4. Feasibility of the solution
5. Practicability / real-world applicability
6. Sustainability
7. Scale of impact
8. User experience
9. Future potential
10. Measurable milestones and timeline

Hackathon juries also evaluate:

- Problem-solution fit
- Working demo / prototype
- Tech choice and architecture
- Team collaboration during the event
- Ability to take feedback

## Idea submission PPT template (official 6-slide structure)

1. **Title slide**
   - Problem Statement ID
   - Problem Statement Title
   - Theme
   - PS Category (Software/Hardware)
   - Team ID / Team Name
2. **Proposed Solution**
   - Detailed explanation
   - How it addresses the problem
   - Innovation and uniqueness
3. **Technical Approach**
   - Technologies used
   - Methodology / flow chart / architecture
   - Working prototype status
4. **Feasibility and Viability**
   - Feasibility analysis
   - Potential challenges and risks
   - Mitigation strategies
5. **Impact and Benefits**
   - Target audience impact
   - Social, economic, environmental benefits
6. **Research and References**
   - Data sources
   - Links / papers / similar solutions

Rules of thumb:
- Max 6 slides including title.
- Avoid paragraphs; use points, diagrams and infographics.
- Keep explanation precise and easy to understand.
- Save as PDF only.
- Do not alter the template pointers.

## Pre-event checklist

### Before internal hackathon

- [ ] Find your college SPOC and join the internal hackathon process.
- [ ] Form a 6-member team with one female member.
- [ ] Choose 3–5 candidate PSs and score each on domain fit, technical fit, data availability and competition.
- [ ] Read the official PS descriptions and expected solutions.
- [ ] Collect datasets, APIs and reference links.
- [ ] Prepare a 6-slide PPT in the official template.
- [ ] Build a basic MVP or wireframe if possible.
- [ ] Assign roles and rehearse the pitch.

### After internal selection

- [ ] Get the NOC/consent letter from your college.
- [ ] Register the team leader and all members on the SIH portal.
- [ ] Submit the idea before the portal deadline.
- [ ] Upload any supporting documents / GitHub link.
- [ ] Monitor the portal for shortlisting.

### Grand finale

- [ ] Carry the printed consent letter, photo IDs and portal printouts.
- [ ] Bring your own laptops, chargers, hardware components and backup drives.
- [ ] Prepare for no internet or unstable internet; keep local builds ready.
- [ ] Pack essentials: clothes, power banks, medicines, snacks.
- [ ] Rehearse the final demo and pitch.
- [ ] Keep the presentation time under the limit.
- [ ] Listen to mentors and iterate after each review.

## Final advice from winners

- "Hackathons are not just about coding; they are about problem-solving, storytelling and impact."
- "A great presentation + prototype + clarity of thought = winning formula."
- "Talk to mentors first; they will tell you what the industry actually needs."
- "A grounded, clear explanation always outperforms an over-polished pitch."
