# Team Formation and Problem Statement Selection

## How to form a team

SIH is not just about coding. A good team has a mix of skills and good chemistry.

### Recommended 6-member team

| Role | Main job |
|------|----------|
| Team Lead / Project Manager | Coordinates, decides, communicates, keeps timeline |
| Lead Developer / Architect | Codes the core, system design, debugging |
| Backend / API / Cloud person | Database, APIs, deployment, cloud setup |
| Frontend / UI-UX person | Figma, web/app UI, user flow |
| Domain / Research person | Understands the problem area, finds data, references |
| Presentation / Storyteller | PPT, pitch, demo flow, final jury presentation |

### Team-building tips

- Pick people you can work with under pressure for 36 hours.
- Diverse skills beat six similar coders.
- A charismatic/articulate presenter matters because the final demo is judged, not just the code.
- Include at least one female member (mandatory).
- All members must be from the same college.
- Identify and use each person's strength instead of everyone doing the same task.

## Problem statement selection

This is the single biggest factor in winning or losing. Past winners say most teams fail here, not at the coding stage.

### Filters to apply before choosing a PS

1. **Domain fit** — Does the team actually understand this area? If the PS is about agriculture, do you know the users and their pain points?
2. **Technical fit** — Can your team realistically build a prototype in the time available?
3. **Clarity of requirements** — A PS with clear technical hooks and expected output is usually better than a vague one.
4. **Data and resources** — Are there datasets, APIs, or existing tools you can use?
5. **Competition** — Problem statements from ISRO, DRDO, technical PSUs or well-defined government bodies can be strong choices.
6. **Submissions count** — Some winners choose a less-crowded PS or switch at the last moment to one with fewer submissions.
7. **Avoid generic PSs** — Overused themes like "AI chatbot for everything" attract too many similar ideas.
8. **Avoid Student Innovation if you can** — Several winners recommend avoiding this category because it is broad and hard to differentiate.

### Practical selection strategy

1. Read the PS at least 3 times. First read = stated need. Second read = implied constraints. Third read = what the evaluator likely expects.
2. Brainstorm approaches and pick the one that can be built within the hackathon time.
3. Check if an MVP can be shown in 36 hours. If not, drop the PS.
4. Look for a "wow factor" while staying realistic.
5. Prefer PSs with a well-defined target audience and measurable impact.

### Strategic tips from winners

- Choose a PS in a domain the team genuinely cares about or has experience in. Passion sustains long hours.
- Look for PSs from technical organizations (ISRO, DRDO, etc.) because the problem is well scoped.
- Generic PSs get a flood of similar ideas and are harder to win.
- If the rules allow, submit to multiple PSs to increase shortlisting chances.
- Some experienced teams pick their "second PS" at the last leg of the process, choosing one with very few submissions.

## Types of PSs

Software PSs are usually about web apps, mobile apps, AI/ML, dashboards, blockchain, cybersecurity, data analytics and cloud platforms.

Hardware PSs usually involve IoT, embedded systems, sensors, robotics, medical devices, smart agriculture hardware and electronic prototypes.

Pick the edition that matches your team's actual capability, not what is trending.
